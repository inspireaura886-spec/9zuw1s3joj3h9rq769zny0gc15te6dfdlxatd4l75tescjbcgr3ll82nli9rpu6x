Before dawn on February 25, 1942—just two days after Japanese submarine I-17 shelled the Ellwood Oil Field near Santa Barbara—coastal radar tracked an unidentified target west of Los Angeles. Air raid sirens triggered a county-wide blackout, and between 3:06 and 4:14 a.m., the 37th Coast Artillery Brigade fired 1,440 rounds of anti-aircraft ammunition into the searchlight-lit sky without hitting a single aircraft. Five civilians died from blackout car crashes and heart attacks. The 1949 Coast Artillery Association and a 1983 Air Force history traced the barrage to a weather balloon sent up around 1:00 a.m. and wartime 'war nerves.'

Was it war nerves and one stray balloon, or something the guns could never hit?
