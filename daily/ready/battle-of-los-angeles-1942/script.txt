Coastal batteries fired fourteen hundred forty rounds at an object over Los Angeles, and hit nothing.
Before dawn on February twenty-fifth, nineteen forty-two, coastal radar tracked
an unidentified target approaching California two days after a Japanese submarine
shelled an oil field near Santa Barbara. Sirens sounded and a blackout began.
But after an hour of gunfire, not a single bomb fell, and not a single plane was hit.
Five civilians died indirectly, in blackout car crashes and from heart attacks.
The next day, Navy Secretary Frank Knox called it a false alarm, while the Army Secretary said as many as fifteen planes had flown over.
A nineteen forty-nine Coast Artillery history blamed a weather balloon for starting the shooting.
And in nineteen eighty-three, an Air Force history called it war nerves, set off by a lost weather balloon.
So was it nerves and one stray balloon, or something the guns could never hit?
