### Official / Mainstream Explanation & Why It Is Still Argued
- **Official / Mainstream Explanation & Why It Is Still Argued:** Both the 1949 U.S. Coast Artillery Association report and the 1983 U.S. Office of Air Force History study—corroborated by Japanese postwar archives showing no Imperial aircraft flew over Los Angeles—concluded that the barrage was a classic case of wartime 'war nerves' two days after the Ellwood Oil Field submarine shelling (`F3, F11, F12, F13`). A meteorological balloon released at 1:00 a.m. triggered the initial sightings (`F6, F11`), after which converging searchlights illuminating smoky anti-aircraft shell bursts convinced gunners they were firing at enemy planes (`F10, F12`). It is still argued in UFO lore because of the conflicting 1942 statements by Navy Secretary Frank Knox and War Secretary Henry Stimson (`F9`) and the heavily paint-retouched *Los Angeles Times* front-page searchlight photo (`F14`).

### What Is Genuinely Still Unexplained
- **What Is Genuinely Still Unexplained:** While the 1949 and 1983 military histories explain the 3:06 a.m. shooting over Santa Monica via a weather balloon (`F6, F11, F12`), wartime logs never pinpointed which specific balloon or atmospheric return produced the initial 1:45 a.m. coastal radar blip 120 miles west of Los Angeles (`F5, F13`).
