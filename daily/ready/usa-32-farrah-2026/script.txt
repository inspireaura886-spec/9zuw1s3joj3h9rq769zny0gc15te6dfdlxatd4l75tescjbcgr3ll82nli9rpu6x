In September twenty twenty-six, a Cold War spy satellite broke apart in orbit, and nobody will say why.
It was called U S A thirty-two, launched in nineteen eighty-eight on a Titan Two rocket.
Analysts identify it as Farrah Three, which listened to Soviet radar. Yes, the name matches the actress.
On September thirteenth, the Space Force confirmed it had fragmented, about seven hundred seventy-five kilometres up.
No cause. No official fragment count.
Online, people asked if it was shot down on purpose. No government has said that.
Space analysts suggest something duller: an old battery or fuel tank may simply have ruptured.
Or a tiny piece of untracked debris hit it, in one of the busiest orbits above Earth.
One modelling firm estimates roughly thirty-five hundred fragments bigger than a centimetre.
So what really shattered a Cold War spy satellite?
