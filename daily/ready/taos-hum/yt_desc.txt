Since around 1991 some residents of Taos, New Mexico have heard a low, throbbing hum, often compared to an idling diesel engine, that most people there cannot hear. In 1993, at the request of New Mexico's congressional delegation, a team from Sandia and Los Alamos national laboratories, the Air Force Phillips Laboratory and the University of New Mexico measured acoustic, seismic, electric, magnetic and electromagnetic signals. They found nothing matching the hum; geophones picked up a tunneling gopher, magnetometers caught lightning and the power grid. Hearers matched the sound to tones of about 33 to 80 Hz. The team's ear researcher ruled out tinnitus in 1993; a 2026 study of 28 hum hearers in Germany suggests low-frequency tinnitus is often the reason, but not in every case.

Is it in the ears, or in the ground?
