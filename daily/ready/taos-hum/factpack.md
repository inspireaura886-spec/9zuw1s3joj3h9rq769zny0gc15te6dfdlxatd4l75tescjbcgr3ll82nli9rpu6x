Verified facts (use only these):
- Since about 1991 some Taos residents heard a continuous sound compared to the throb of a diesel engine; most Taos residents don't hear it (LA Times 1993); idling diesel engine (Herald 2004; PLOS One 2026 intro).
- 1993: at the request of NM's congressional delegation, a team from Sandia and Los Alamos national labs, the Air Force Phillips Laboratory and the University of New Mexico measured acoustic, seismic, electric, magnetic and EM signals; no signals were found matching the description (OSTI/JASA 1994 abstract; LA Times 1993).
- Geophones picked up a gopher tunneling, no seismic activity; magnetometers detected lightning and the power grid (LA Times 1993).
- Hearers matched the sound to 33-80 Hz (LA Times 1993); 32-80 Hz (Wikipedia, citing Mullins & Kelly 1995); 30-100 Hz implicated (OSTI abstract).
- 1994 survey: 1,440 responses, 161 heard the hum (Herald 2004). 'At least two percent' (Wikipedia/ASA) - not voiced.
- Team ear researcher Joe (James) Kelly ruled out tinnitus in 1993 (LA Times 1993).
- 2026 PLOS One study of 28 hearers (Germany/Norway): most had normal low-frequency thresholds, no low-frequency otoacoustic emissions; suggests low-frequency tinnitus is often the reason, external sources not ruled out (PLOS One; Popular Mechanics 2026).
