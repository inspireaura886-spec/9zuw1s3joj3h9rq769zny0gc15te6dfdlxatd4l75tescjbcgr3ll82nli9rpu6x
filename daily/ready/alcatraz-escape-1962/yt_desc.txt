On the night of June 11, 1962, inmates Frank Morris and brothers John and Clarence Anglin escaped from Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary in San Francisco Bay after placing painted papier-mâché dummy heads topped with real human hair in their beds. Having widened their cell vents over six months, they climbed to the roof and launched an inflatable raft stitched from more than 50 raincoats. Although the FBI closed its case in December 1979 concluding the men likely drowned, a 2014 Delft University tide study found that a raft launched between 11:30 p.m. and midnight could have reached Horseshoe Bay (a window of about 30 minutes), and a 2013 letter signed 'John Anglin' was found inconclusive by FBI handwriting, fingerprint and DNA tests.

Did freezing bay currents drown the three men, or did they catch the 11:30 p.m. tide to Horseshoe Bay?
