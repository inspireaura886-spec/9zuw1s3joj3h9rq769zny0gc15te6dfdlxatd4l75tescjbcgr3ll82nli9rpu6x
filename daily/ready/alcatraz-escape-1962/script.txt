Three prisoners vanished from Alcatraz, leaving painted dummy heads with real hair in their beds.
On June eleventh, nineteen sixty-two, Frank Morris and brothers John and Clarence
Anglin squeezed through widened cell vents inside the San Francisco Bay
prison and paddled away on a raft stitched from more than fifty stolen raincoats.
But a two thousand fourteen Delft University tide model found a narrow thirty-minute launch window that could reach shore.
A raft leaving between eleven thirty p.m. and midnight could have reached Horseshoe Bay, just north of the Golden Gate Bridge.
In two thousand thirteen, San Francisco police received a letter signed John Anglin, claiming all three made it.
The FBI's handwriting, fingerprint and DNA tests on it were inconclusive. Its official finding, in nineteen seventy-nine, was that the men drowned.
Did freezing currents drown the trio, or did they escape after fooling guards at their beds?
