A satellite photo showed what looked like Amelia Earhart's plane, sitting in a Pacific lagoon.
It was first spotted in twenty twenty, on the remote atoll of Nikumaroro.
Then it turned up in aerial photos from nineteen thirty-eight, barely a year after she vanished.
In October twenty twenty-six, a seventeen-person team sailed five days to find out.
They surveyed the lagoon with a drone, sonar, and a magnetometer that detects steel.
On October nineteenth, they announced the answer: it was not her plane. It was a clump of fallen trees.
Most of it had already washed away. Only one Y-shaped piece was left.
And the metal shine? A hardened crust of coral sand.
They did pull a thirty-inch steel tube from the lagoon, but nobody has tied it to her Electra.
Eighty-nine years on, no confirmed wreckage. So where did Amelia Earhart go?
