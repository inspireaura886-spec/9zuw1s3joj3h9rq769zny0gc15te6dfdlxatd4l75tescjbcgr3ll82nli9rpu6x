On 11 July 1897 Swedish engineer S. A. Andrée, Knut Frænkel and Nils Strindberg lifted off from Danes Island, Svalbard, in the hydrogen balloon Örnen (Eagle) and were never seen again for 33 years. In August 1930 the Norwegian sealing vessel Bratvaag found their final camp on Kvitøya; in September the sloop Isbjørn found more remains and a tin box of film. Of 240 exposed frames, 93 were saved and developed in Stockholm. The men's deaths, a few days after reaching the island, still have no agreed cause: trichinosis, a polar bear, carbon monoxide, morphine and more have been argued.
