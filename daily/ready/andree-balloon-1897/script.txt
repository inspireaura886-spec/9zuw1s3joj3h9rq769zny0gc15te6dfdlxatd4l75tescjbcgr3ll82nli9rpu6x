Three men floated toward the North Pole in a hydrogen balloon, and vanished into the ice.
On July eleventh, eighteen ninety-seven, Salomon Andree launched the silk balloon Eagle from Svalbard, with Knut Fraenkel and photographer Nils Strindberg.
For thirty-three years, nobody knew what happened to them.
Then, in August nineteen thirty, Norwegian hunters landed on a bare island called Kvitoya, and found their camp.
They found a boathook engraved with Andree's name, a journal, and two skeletons.
A month later, a second ship found the third man, and a tin box of exposed film.
A chemist in Stockholm saved ninety-three photographs from two hundred forty frames.
They showed the balloon crashed on the ice after about two days in the air.
Unhurt, the men trekked nearly two hundred fifty miles, until their floating camp broke apart in October.
Within days of reaching that island, all three were dead. So what killed them?
