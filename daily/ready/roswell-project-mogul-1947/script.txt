The Army Air Forces announced they had recovered a crashed flying disc, and the next day they took it back.
On July eighth, nineteen forty-seven, Roswell Army Air Field issued
a press release after rancher Mac Brazel found tinfoil, rubber,
and wooden sticks scattered across a New Mexico ranch.
But the next day, General Roger Ramey told reporters the wreckage was a weather balloon.
In nineteen seventy-eight, Major Jesse Marcel told a UFO researcher that balloon story was a cover story.
Later books added alien bodies and a government cover-up.
In nineteen ninety-four, an Air Force report concluded the debris most likely came from Project Mogul, a secret balloon project.
Mogul's balloon trains were built to listen for Soviet atomic bomb tests. Even the purple symbols Marcel's son recalled matched flower-printed tape from a New York toy maker.
So was it a saucer, or a secret the Army hid behind a weather balloon?
