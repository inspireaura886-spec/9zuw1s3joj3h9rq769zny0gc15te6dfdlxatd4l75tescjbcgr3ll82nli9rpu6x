### Official / Mainstream Explanation & Why It Is Still Argued
- **Official / Mainstream Explanation & Why It Is Still Argued:** As established by the 1994 and 1997 U.S. Air Force reports (*The Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction in the New Mexico Desert* and *Case Closed*), the tinfoil, rubber, balsa sticks, and flower-printed tape found by rancher Mac Brazel in June 1947 were wreckage from **Project Mogul Flight 4**—a classified train of high-altitude balloons and radar reflectors launched from Alamogordo on 4 June 1947 to listen for Soviet atomic tests (`F3, F9, F10, F11, F12`). The July 1947 'weather balloon' press conference was a real cover story designed to protect top-secret Project Mogul (`F5, F8, F16`), while 1980s 'alien body' stories conflated the 1948 Aztec hoax, 1950s anthropomorphic crash-test dummies, and 1950s air crashes (`F13, F14, F15`). It remains argued in popular culture because the Army's own 8 July 1947 'flying disc' press release and 47-year secrecy around Project Mogul fueled lasting conspiracy lore (`F1, F2, F7, F10`).

### What Is Genuinely Still Unexplained
- **What Is Genuinely Still Unexplained:** Nothing physical about the June 1947 ranch debris remains unexplained—it matched Project Mogul Flight 4 down to the New York toy-company tape (`F9, F11, F12`). The only enduring historical question is why the 509th Bomb Group's intelligence and public information officers issued an official 'flying disc' press release on 8 July 1947 before higher headquarters recognized the classified Alamogordo balloon train (`F1, F2, F4, F5`).
