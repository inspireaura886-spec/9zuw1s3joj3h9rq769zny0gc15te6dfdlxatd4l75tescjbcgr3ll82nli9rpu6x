On Oak Island in Nova Scotia, treasure hunters have dug at the so-called Money Pit since the late 1700s. According to accounts first published in the 1800s, early diggers hit log platforms about every ten feet; at about 90 feet the Onslow company reported a flat stone with strange symbols, and at 98 feet something hard, but by the next morning the pit held about 60 feet of water. In 1909 a young Franklin D. Roosevelt took part in an expedition there. No treasure has been recovered. Geologists point out that the island's gypsum and limestone bedrock forms natural sinkholes, and say the Money Pit may be one, with the "platforms" being fallen trees and the flooding natural. Others say that leaves the finds at Smith's Cove unexplained.
