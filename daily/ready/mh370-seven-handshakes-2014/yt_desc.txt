On 8 March 2014, Malaysia Airlines Flight MH370, a Boeing 777 registered 9M-MRO, vanished with 239 people aboard on its way from Kuala Lumpur to Beijing. Its last radio call came at 1:19 a.m.; two minutes later its transponder stopped. Military radar watched it turn back across Malaysia for about another hour. After that, only an Inmarsat satellite link remained: seven automated "handshakes" with a ground station in Perth, the last at 8:19 a.m. Each gave only a distance from the satellite, and the final ring crossed the southern Indian Ocean. A flaperon from its wing washed up on Réunion in 2015. Searches have covered more than 120,000 square kilometres of seafloor without finding the aircraft, and the cause has never been determined. In June 2026 Malaysia extended its "no find, no fee" search deal with Ocean Infinity to 30 June 2027, with 7,428.54 sq km left to search.
